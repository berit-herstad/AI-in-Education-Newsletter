The day AI safety moved from school policy to platform accountability.

A British Columbia lawsuit pushed school AI risk beyond classroom rules and into platform duty-of-care. At the same time, schools kept building practical AI literacy systems: governance committees, source trails, teacher mentorship, and safer pilots.

1Top story
Canada · AI safety

A province asks whether an AI platform has a duty to warn.

The Guardian reports that British Columbia has sued OpenAI and Sam Altman after a school shooting, alleging the company should have warned law enforcement when dangerous conversations were flagged. Ars Technica says the complaint seeks damages and independent audits, and asks the court to change model instructions around violent chats.

The education significance is not the litigation alone. It reframes school AI safety as a platform-accountability question: what must an AI provider do when risk signals appear outside the school firewall, before harm reaches a campus?

School AI safety is no longer only about what happens inside school systems.
2Research & evidence

Evidence is shifting toward audit trails and adult judgment.

AI search is not automatically safer for students.
EdTech Innovation Hub reported on a Youth AI Safety Institute assessment that gave Perplexity its lowest risk rating for children, citing weak age-appropriate behavior, homework answer-giving, and uneven source quality.
Teacher professional learning can embed AI literacy quietly.
Edutopia argued that schools can build teacher AI literacy through small routines: showing prompts, citing AI-generated images, modeling tool use, and sharing AI-assisted materials transparently.
Source-backed inquiry remains a promising assessment pattern.
Donga Science showed how teachers can review question trails, sources, and hypotheses when students use Science DongA Air Edu, shifting evidence from finished product to process.

6Worth watching

Three patterns to track.

Duty-of-care will move upstream.
School AI risk will increasingly involve platforms, law enforcement thresholds, and auditability—not only district policies.
AI literacy is becoming governance literacy.
Schools are connecting prompts and tool skills with parent consent, data rules, source quality, and risk classification.
Process evidence is becoming the bridge.
Source trails, teacher dashboards, oral defenses, and inquiry logs all turn AI use into something educators can inspect.
