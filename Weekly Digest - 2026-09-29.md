# The week AI readiness stopped meaning tool access.

The strongest signal is that schools are defining AI readiness as governance: curriculum, assessment, teacher capacity, procurement standards, and student-safe defaults. The question is shifting from who gets AI to what system surrounds it.

## 1. Top Story

**School leaders define AI readiness as an operating model, not a lab.** At an <u>[ET Education](https://education.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/ai-ready-schools-principals-call-for-structured-governance-teacher-mentorship-and-student-led-innovation-at-eteducation-roundtable/134387059)</u> roundtable in Gurugram, more than 30 school leaders argued that AI readiness cannot be reduced to a checklist, chatbot, or AI lab. They called for leadership vision, curriculum alignment, teacher capacity, assessment reform, and ethical safeguards. The most useful detail was organizational: governance committees, written policies, risk classifications, AI declaration forms, and incident protocols.

## 2. Research & Evidence

**AI readiness needs roles, rules, and rubrics.** <u>[ET Education](https://education.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/ai-ready-schools-principals-call-for-structured-governance-teacher-mentorship-and-student-led-innovation-at-eteducation-roundtable/134387059)</u> says teachers need to mentor, question AI-supported answers, and evaluate reasoning.
