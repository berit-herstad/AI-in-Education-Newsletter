The day AI policy turned into operating rules.

This issue’s through-line is governance becoming operational: who can use AI, when, how it is assessed, and how teachers are trained.

1Top story
Brazil · National guidance

Brazil moves AI in education from adoption to risk governance.

Brazil’s National Education Council approved national guidelines for AI use across schools and universities, a notable step because it treats education AI as a risk-governance problem rather than a procurement trend. Reporting from Porvir, Correio Braziliense, and Tecnoblog says the rules create risk categories and limits on sensitive uses such as automated essay correction, with institutions expected to adapt after ministry homologation.

Brazil is moving the debate from “AI in the classroom” to “which educational decisions are too important to automate.”

5Worth watching

Three patterns to track.

Risk categories are becoming the policy language.
Brazil’s framework, EU-style risk thinking, and district age gates all move beyond broad “AI allowed/banned” rules.
Teacher training is the new adoption layer.
India and the UAE show that curriculum rollout now comes with educator enablement.
Assessment reform is spreading faster than detection.
Higher education is moving toward proof-of-process instead of policing finished text.
