# The week AI safety widened beyond school rules.

The clearest pattern is no longer whether schools allow AI. Courts, provinces, school systems, and platform evaluators are all asking who is responsible for age-appropriate defaults, evidence trails, and student-safe design.

## 1. Top Story

**British Columbia keeps school AI safety in the courts.** <u>[The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/22/british-columbia-sues-openai-sam-altman-tumbler-ridge-school-shooting)</u> and <u>[Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/lawsuit-demands-openai-pay-for-new-school-after-chatgpt-used-in-shooting/)</u> reported that British Columbia sued OpenAI and Sam Altman, alleging the company should have warned law enforcement when dangerous conversations were flagged before a school shooting. The case moves the safety boundary upstream: classroom rules matter, but so do platform escalation duties, audits, and model instructions.

## 2. Research & Evidence

**AI search still needs student-safe defaults.** <u>[EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/perplexitys-ai-search-isnt-a-safer-choice-for-kids-than-google-youth-ai-safety-institute-finds)</u> reported that Perplexity was not a safer choice for children than Google.

**AI-ready schools need governance maturity.** <u>[ET Education](https://education.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/ai-ready-schools-principals-call-for-structured-governance-teacher-mentorship-and-student-led-innovation-at-eteducation-roundtable/134387059)</u> says Indian school leaders called for policies, committees, risk classifications, and teacher mentorship.
