# The day AI safety moved from school policy to platform accountability.

A British Columbia lawsuit pushed school AI risk beyond classroom rules and into platform duty-of-care. At the same time, schools kept building practical AI literacy systems: governance committees, source trails, teacher mentorship, and safer pilots.

## 1. Top Story

**British Columbia sued OpenAI over school-safety duties.** <u>[The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/22/british-columbia-sues-openai-sam-altman-tumbler-ridge-school-shooting)</u> reports that the province alleges the company should have warned law enforcement when dangerous conversations were flagged before a school shooting. <u>[Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/lawsuit-demands-openai-pay-for-new-school-after-chatgpt-used-in-shooting/)</u> says the complaint seeks damages, independent audits, and changes to model instructions around violent chats. The education significance is broader than the case: school AI safety is becoming a platform-accountability question.

## 2. Research & Evidence

**AI search is not automatically safer for students.** <u>[EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/perplexitys-ai-search-isnt-a-safer-choice-for-kids-than-google-youth-ai-safety-institute-finds)</u> reported that the Youth AI Safety Institute gave Perplexity its lowest risk rating for children, citing weak age-appropriate behavior and uneven sources.

**Teacher learning can embed AI literacy in small routines.** <u>[Edutopia](https://www.edutopia.org/article/sharing-ai-literacy-tips-teachers-professional-learning)</u> argues for modeling prompts, citations, and AI-assisted materials inside ordinary professional learning.

**Source-backed inquiry remains a useful pattern.** <u>[Donga Science](https://www.dongascience.com/en/news/79939)</u> showed how teachers can inspect question trails and source use, not only final answers.

## 5. Tools & Platforms

AI search now needs a student safety layer; school pilots are choosing purpose-built tools with data controls; and professional learning is turning AI literacy into small, repeatable teaching habits.

## 6. Worth Watching

Duty-of-care is moving upstream to platforms; AI literacy is becoming governance literacy; and process evidence remains the bridge between AI access and trustworthy learning.
