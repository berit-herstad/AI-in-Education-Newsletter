# Schools are demanding AI controls without losing core search access

This week, education AI policy moved from abstract guardrails to operational design: districts want tighter student protections, but not at the cost of basic research access and classroom continuity.

## 1. Top Story
Large U.S. districts are reportedly reassessing search strategy as Google’s school controls tie AI settings and Search access together, turning configuration into a core governance decision. <u>[Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/googles-ai-controls-have-major-districts-rethinking-search/)</u>. Google says Guided Learning in Search is designed to support productive struggle and critical thinking. <u>[Google Blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-learning-era-updates-2026/)</u>.

## 2. Research & Evidence
- Stanford-linked coverage of a 52-study causal review reports mixed learning gains and warns that cognitive offloading can weaken unassisted performance. <u>[CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/stanford-scale-ai-k12-causal-evidence-review-november-2026)</u>.
- ThinkYoung survey coverage shows a guidance gap: high student AI homework use, lower rates of formal instruction on safe and reliable use. <u>[EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/openai-calls-for-teacher-training-as-student-ai-homework-use-outpaces-guidance)</u>.
- Tamil Nadu expanded AI-supported learning to 25,140 students in 500 government schools with teacher-led implementation. <u>[DigitalLEARNING](https://digitallearning.eletsonline.com/2026/11/tamil-nadu-ai-learning-schools/)</u>.
