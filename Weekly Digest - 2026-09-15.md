# The day unclear AI policy became the story

Today’s through-line is clarity. After a week of safety standards, teacher guides, curriculum rollouts, and student restrictions, the biggest signal is that schools still do not have policies teachers can actually use.

## Top Story

<u>[The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/stanford-ai-index-2026-education)</u> reported a sharp finding from Stanford’s AI Index education coverage: only 6% of teachers say their school’s AI policy is clear, while four in five US students are using AI for schoolwork and about half of schools have no policy. The story also notes that adoption is accelerating outside the West.

That makes “policy clarity” the missing layer between ambition and practice. This month’s news has shown national frameworks, vendor safety standards, and teacher training, but teachers still need usable answers: what is allowed, what must be disclosed, what counts as learning evidence, and what data protections are guaranteed.

> The problem is no longer whether AI has entered school. It is whether adults can explain the rules.

## Research & Evidence
