# Schools are moving from AI adoption to AI assurance

This week, the strongest education-AI signal is a governance shift: tighter controls on student AI access and tougher assessment designs to prove real learning across K-12, higher education, and skills pathways.

## 1. Top Story
England’s higher-ed regulator says AI has made some coursework assessment “close to impossible” and is pushing universities toward in-person exams, vivas, and process-based evidence. <u>[The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/education/2026/nov/11/ai-close-to-impossible-assess-university-coursework-regulator)</u>, <u>[Yahoo News Australia](https://au.news.yahoo.com/ai-now-universal-higher-education-184042276.html)</u>.

## 2. Research & Evidence
- Stanford’s November evidence update across 52 causal studies finds mixed learning gains, with retention risk when students offload core work to AI. <u>[Stanford SCALE](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Evidence%20Base%20on%20AI%20in%20K-12%20Report.pdf)</u>.
- YoLearn.ai launched a curriculum-aligned K-12 model (Pragya) across 12+ Indian languages for partner schools. <u>[DigitalLEARNING](https://digitallearning.eletsonline.com/2026/11/yolearn-pragya-llm/)</u>.
- Tamil Nadu expanded AI-supported learning to 25,140 public-school students in 500 middle schools. <u>[DigitalLEARNING](https://digitallearning.eletsonline.com/2026/11/tamil-nadu-ai-learning-schools/)</u>.
