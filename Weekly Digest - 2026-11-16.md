# AI assurance is becoming policy default in education

This week’s clearest shift is from experimentation to enforcement: schools and universities are tightening AI controls, redesigning assessment, and scaling teacher-facing implementation support.

## 1. Top Story
England’s Office for Students says AI has made some coursework assessment “close to impossible,” pushing universities toward in-person exams, vivas, and process-based formats. <u>[The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/education/2026/nov/11/ai-close-to-impossible-assess-university-coursework-regulator)</u>, <u>[Yahoo News Australia](https://au.news.yahoo.com/ai-now-universal-higher-education-184042276.html)</u>.

## 2. Research & Evidence
- Stanford’s November evidence update finds mixed learning gains and weaker unassisted retention when students offload core work to AI. <u>[Stanford SCALE](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Evidence%20Base%20on%20AI%20in%20K-12%20Report.pdf)</u>.
- India’s Pragya model rollout shows curriculum-aligned, local-language K-12 tutoring scaling quickly. <u>[DigitalLEARNING](https://digitallearning.eletsonline.com/2026/11/yolearn-pragya-llm/)</u>.
- Tamil Nadu expanded AI-supported public-school learning to 25,140 students in 500 middle schools. <u>[DigitalLEARNING](https://digitallearning.eletsonline.com/2026/11/tamil-nadu-ai-learning-schools/)</u>.
