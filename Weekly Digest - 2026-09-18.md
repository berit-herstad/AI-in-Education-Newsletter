# The day higher ed moved from AI policy to redesign

Today’s through-line is redesign. K-12 systems are still tightening rules, but higher education is now asking the deeper question: if AI changes how students learn, prove, and research, what has to change in the institution itself?

## Top Story

<u>[Government Technology](https://www.govtech.com/education/higher-ed/mit-report-calls-for-ai-aware-redesign-of-higher-ed)</u> reports that MIT’s new AI report calls for universities to rethink teaching, campus life, and research training as generative AI reshapes student and instructor work. <u>[The Tech](https://thetech.com/2026/09/17/ai-committee-report)</u> notes the report is MIT’s first step toward an institute-wide response after nine months of committee work.

The significance is scale. This is not a course-level AI policy or an academic-integrity memo; it is a signal that universities may need coordinated redesign across assessment, instruction, research practice, student support, and human skills. It also connects directly to the K-12 “proof of learning” debate: if AI can credibly complete work, institutions need better evidence of thinking.

> The question is shifting from “How should students use AI?” to “What should an AI-aware institution become?”
