# The day AI literacy became a trust gap

Today’s clearest signal is not a new model or school ban: it is confidence. New survey coverage shows educators increasingly believe they can teach about AI, while parents still need clearer assurance that classroom use will support learning rather than replace it.

## Top Story

A new <u>[Education Week](https://www.edweek.org/technology/educators-feel-confident-they-can-teach-about-ai-what-do-parents-think/2026/09)</u> report on an IBM-commissioned Morning Consult survey found 83% of educators say they feel confident teaching about AI. That matters because the policy debate is quickly becoming an implementation debate: after months of bans, curriculum launches, and teacher-training announcements, systems now need adults who can explain what students are doing, why it matters, and where the boundaries sit.

The unresolved question is parent trust. If parents see AI as shortcut technology, schools will struggle to move beyond defensive rules. If they see teachers using it to strengthen feedback, practice, accessibility, and AI literacy, the same tools become part of basic preparation for modern work.

> The next bottleneck is not access to AI. It is whether families trust schools to teach with it responsibly.

## Worth Watching

Trust is becoming the adoption layer: educator confidence is rising, but parent confidence will determine whether AI literacy becomes routine.

AI infrastructure and education are converging, visible in Paraguay’s tutor/data-center story and platform-led national training efforts.

Assessment redesign is becoming the durable answer to cheating anxiety: less detection, more demonstrated process.
