# AI training is outpacing AI pedagogy in professional education

This week’s signals point to a new phase: institutions are rapidly expanding AI use, but now face pressure to redesign teaching, assessment, and governance so adoption stays credible.

## 1. Top Story
A Yale-led national survey in family-medicine residency found that 71% of residents had used AI for work in the prior month, while only 36% reported recent training. That gap makes AI governance a curriculum issue, not just a policy one. <u>[Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-medical-residents-ai.html)</u>.

## 2. Research & Evidence
- The same study reinforces a core shift: institutions now need structured instruction on when and how AI should be used in professional judgment. <u>[Family Medicine](https://journals.stfm.org/familymedicine/online-first/barr-0347/)</u>.
- UNESCO’s Digital Transformation Collaborative work points to system-level AI implementation and governance as the next stage after pilot activity. <u>[UNESCO via Bing News RSS](https://www.bing.com/news/search?q=UNESCO+AI+education+October+2026&format=rss)</u>.
- In India, a national workshop led by BHU, IIT Kanpur, and CSJMU focused directly on teaching and assessment redesign for AI-rich classrooms. <u>[Jagran Josh](https://www.jagranjosh.com/news/bhu-iit-kanpur-csjmu-to-hold-national-workshop-on-ai-in-higher-education-on-october-24-check-details-188437)</u>.

## 6. Worth Watching
- Assessment integrity standards will likely tighten around process evidence, not just final outputs.
- Procurement language (audit rights, breach reporting, patch windows) is becoming central to educational AI quality.
- Executive AI literacy is becoming a bottleneck for institutional scaling decisions.
