# The day school AI risk moved upstream.

Education systems are no longer treating AI safety as only a classroom-management problem. Platform accountability, age-appropriate AI search, and formal AI literacy frameworks are moving closer to the center of school policy.

## 1. Top Story

**British Columbia sued OpenAI over school-safety duties.** <u>[The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/22/british-columbia-sues-openai-sam-altman-tumbler-ridge-school-shooting)</u> reports that the province alleges the company should have warned law enforcement when dangerous ChatGPT conversations were flagged before a school shooting. <u>[Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/lawsuit-demands-openai-pay-for-new-school-after-chatgpt-used-in-shooting/)</u> says the complaint seeks damages, independent audits, and model-instruction changes.

## 2. Research & Evidence

**AI search is not automatically safer for children.** <u>[EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/perplexitys-ai-search-isnt-a-safer-choice-for-kids-than-google-youth-ai-safety-institute-finds)</u> reported that the Youth AI Safety Institute gave Perplexity its lowest child-risk rating.

**Teacher learning can carry AI literacy in small routines.** <u>[Edutopia](https://www.edutopia.org/article/sharing-ai-literacy-tips-teachers-professional-learning)</u> argues for modeling prompts, citations, and transparent AI-assisted materials inside ordinary professional learning.

## 5. Tools & Platforms

AI search needs student defaults; school pilots are choosing purpose-built tools with data controls; and professional learning is turning AI literacy into repeatable teacher habits.

## 6. Worth Watching

Platform duty-of-care will shape school procurement. AI literacy is becoming governance literacy. Process evidence remains the trust layer between AI access and credible learning.
