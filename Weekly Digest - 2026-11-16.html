Schools are moving from AI adoption to AI assurance

This week, the strongest education-AI signal is a governance shift: tighter controls on student AI access and tougher assessment designs to prove real learning across K-12, higher education, and skills pathways.

1Top story
United Kingdom · Higher Education

England’s regulator says AI is making some coursework assessment “close to impossible”

England’s Office for Students warns universities that failing to maintain valid, reliable assessment in the AI era could breach registration conditions, and is pointing institutions toward more in-person exams, vivas, and process-based formats. The Guardian.

The pressure is broader than the UK: Australian higher-ed analysis says student AI use is now near-universal while many institutions still lack clear policy and assessment redesign depth, reinforcing a global implementation gap. Yahoo News Australia.

The core policy question has shifted from whether students use AI to how institutions verify what students actually know.
2Research & evidence

Research & Evidence

Stanford’s expanded causal evidence review shows mixed learning effects from AI
A November update covering 52 causal studies reports that AI can improve task completion and confidence, but unassisted retention is inconsistent when students offload core cognitive work. Stanford SCALE.
India is scaling classroom AI with curriculum alignment
YoLearn.ai launched its Pragya model for classes 1–8 in multiple Indian languages, positioned as NEP/NCF-aligned tutoring for 20,000 partner schools. DigitalLEARNING.
State rollout data from Tamil Nadu shows AI in public-school operations
Tamil Nadu launched AI-supported self-learning for 25,140 students across 500 government middle schools, emphasizing teacher-guided use and student-level progress tracking. DigitalLEARNING.
